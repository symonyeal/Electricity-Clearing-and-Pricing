# Clearing and prices

For unit g and period t, p is generation, u is commitment, v is start-up and w is shut-down.
The offer consists of energy cost c, no-load cost n and start-up cost s. The generator set
X includes minimum output, capacity, ramps, start/stop restrictions, minimum run/down
times, state transitions and initial-state obligations. `unit::rows` defines X once.

The clearing minimizes the sum of c p + n u + s v over X, subject to nodal balance and
network limits. DC lines obey angle differences and symmetric capacities. Directional
transport exchanges obey their signed capacity pairs without a loop-flow condition.
[Sources](SOURCES.md) records the formulations.

## Price rules

| Route | Priced feasible set |
| --- | --- |
| `lmp` | Dispatch with the integer clearing's commitment fixed |
| `chp` | Product of exact generator hulls, represented by interval graphs |
| `hl` | The same hull, represented by the union of trajectory polytopes |
| `cg` | Extreme-point master generated by per-unit self-scheduling oracles |
| `rx` | Continuous relaxation of the original commitment rows |
| `aic` | Exact hull after capping the cleared blocks that lose money at LMP |

Each price is the dual of its program's balance equations. Commitment is cleared once
for comparisons of payment rules. AIC starts with each unit's full capacity; a losing
on-block is capped at its cleared dispatch plus epsilon, default 1e-6 MW. Periods with
cleared output at most 1e-7 MW are capped at zero.

The interval formulation has O(T^2) candidate arcs and O(T^3) dispatch variables per
unit. The trajectory formulation enumerates up to 2^T schedules and is retained as a
mathematically distinct native validation route. Column generation requires an exactly
repriced final dual; a restricted-master objective alone does not certify that price.

## Uplift and bounds

At price pi, realized profit is energy revenue less operating cost. Make-whole is the
sum of negative commitment-block profits, with sign reversed. Total uplift is the best
profit available by self-scheduling minus realized profit; lost opportunity is total
uplift less make-whole. `pricing::pay` computes the split.

Relaxing nodal balance leaves both generator and network subproblems. The Lagrangian
value is demand payment minus maximum generator profits plus the minimum network term.
Its value bounds clearing cost from below. On a congested network, omitting the network
term can instead put the purported bound above the clearing cost.

The one-bus identity uplift = clearing cost - Lagrangian value requires a congestion-rent
correction on a network. At arbitrary nodal prices, add both the minimum network term
and demand payment minus the revenue of the cleared generation. The network term and
that rent cancel only at an appropriate
dual optimum. Solver bounds, rather than incumbents, enter every Lagrangian certificate.

## Validation cases

| Case | Construction | Pinned result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Hua-Baldick, one period | LMP 50/MWh; CHP 12/MWh |
| 2 | Hua-Baldick, three periods and ramping | CHP (60, 60, 65.6)/MWh |
| 3 | Chen-O'Neill-Whitman FERC case | Clearing 7,340; peak LMP 90, CHP 276, full p-cut AIC 422/MWh |
| 4 | Synthetic equal-reactance three-bus loop | Clearing 2,100; hull nodal prices (10, 30, 50)/MWh |
| 5 | Synthetic two-zone offers and demand | Directional exchange bounds [-3500, 2200] MW; clearing 275,000 |

Case 3's LMP uplift is 1,690; its CHP uplift is 365, with no make-whole.
The FERC talk reports an AIC price of 146.33/MWh. Restricting the second unit to the
cleared and off schedules gives that value; the full p-cut hull used here gives 422.
These are different feasible sets. The talk does not establish that its earlier AIC
row used the later AIC34 cuts, so its number is not treated as the unrestricted answer.

Price coordinates can be nonunique even when value and demand payment agree. The
selection rule and numerical qualifications are stated in [Design](DESIGN.md), and
the measured comparison scope in [Validation](VALIDATION.md).
